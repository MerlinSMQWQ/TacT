# Delegation across agent levels

Use this before delegating authorized work, guiding a delegate, or integrating delegated results. Skip it when the task does not involve delegation. This guidance does not grant permission to spawn agents or message another task; the current authorization and tool rules still apply.

## Give a bounded task and enough context

The delegator passes the outcome needed, relevant facts and confirmed constraints, authority and limits, current evidence, and what result will help integration. Identify consequential dependencies or shared work that affect the task. Do not require the delegate to repeat an investigation whose usable results are already available, or assume it has context its runtime does not provide.

The delegate obtains readily available facts itself, advances ordinary work within scope, and asks about a missing decision only after narrowing its consequences. An assigned subtask is not authority to expand the overall goal or make external commitments. If its premises change, it reports the change promptly and continues unaffected work.

## Keep guidance and evidence useful

The delegate selects the few facts most likely to affect the delegator's understanding and next judgment: what works, what was actually checked, the decisive evidence, unresolved obstacles, and choices that need intervention. Preserve traceable evidence such as files, relevant lines, commands and results, or a short record of an experiment when they are needed to assess the conclusion. A log dump makes the delegator perform the filtering again; an unsupported completion claim removes the means to assess it.

The delegator observes meaningful progress and intervenes when the task drifts, attempts stop producing evidence, or supporting work displaces the assigned outcome. It supplies new context and direction when available. This does not require a fixed polling schedule or approval at every step. A useful partial result should arrive when it can change integration or a decision, without abandoning independent work that remains possible.

## Integrate before claiming completion

The delegator owns the assembled delivery. Read the result, reconcile it with the agreed behavior and other work, resolve inconsistencies, and verify the key conclusions that affect delivery. The necessary checks depend on impact, risk, and the quality and applicability of the evidence already supplied. Do not merely forward “done,” and do not mechanically redo every check whose evidence remains valid.

A delegate's passing targeted checks support that scope. They do not establish an untested integration with callers or other delegated changes. If later integration changes invalidate earlier evidence, run the relevant verification again or keep the gap explicit. Carry unresolved matters into the delivery claim where they affect acceptance.

## Why the responsibility remains with the delegator

Delegation extends the developer–agent relationship across levels: enough autonomy to advance ordinary work, enough visibility to intervene in consequential decisions, and evidence that remains available for review. Its value depends on total delivery and coordination costs, including the delegator's attention. Splitting work is not automatically faster or easier to understand.

This first edition introduces no general rules for parallel editing, mandatory reviewer roles, or fixed review ceremonies. Choose a workflow only when the authorized task and actual environment justify it; do not turn this document into a new coordination system.
